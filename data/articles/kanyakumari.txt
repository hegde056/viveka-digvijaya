At the very end of India, where the three seas meet, Swami Vivekananda swam across the shark-infested waters to a rocky island. He sat there for three days and nights—December 25, 26, and 27, 1892.

He did not meditate on God or abstract philosophy. Instead, he meditated on the "current condition of India." He saw the poverty, the suffering, and the downfall of a once-great civilization.

It was here that he found his mission: "To serve God is to serve the poor." He resolved to go to the West not to seek charity, but to earn the means to uplift the masses of India. This moment transformed him from a wandering monk into a global reformer.
