"Sisters and Brothers of America,"

With these five simple words, Swami Vivekananda rose to his feet at the Parliament of the World's Religions on September 11, 1893. The audience of 7,000 people gave him a standing ovation that lasted for two minutes.

He spoke not of a specific dogma, but of universal acceptance. He quoted the Gita: "As the different streams having their sources in different places all mingle their water in the sea, so, O Lord, the different paths which men take through different tendencies, various though they appear, crooked or straight, all lead to Thee."

This speech effectively introduced Hinduism and Yoga to the Western world, moving it from a "heathen superstition" to a respected philosophy in the eyes of the American public.
